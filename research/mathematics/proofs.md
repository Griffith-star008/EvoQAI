# Formal Proofs

## Proof 1: Convergence of Self-Evolving DAG Optimization (Theorem 1)

**Given:** Assumptions 1–5 hold. The algorithm deploys permutation $\pi_t$ at step $t$ only if $\mathcal{L}(\pi_t(\mathcal{G}_t), \mathcal{H}_t) < \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_t, \mathcal{H}_t)$.

**Proof:**

**(Part 1: Monotonicity)**
By the deployment condition, each accepted permutation satisfies:
$$\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_{t+1}, \mathcal{H}_{t+1}) = \mathcal{L}(\pi_t(\mathcal{G}_t), \mathcal{H}_t) < \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_t, \mathcal{H}_t)$$
(using Assumption 4: $\mathcal{H}_{t+1} \approx \mathcal{H}_t$ during a single cycle). Therefore the sequence $\{\mathcal{L}_t\}$ is strictly monotonically decreasing.

**(Part 2: Boundedness)**
By Lemma 3, $\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{H}) \geq 0$ for all valid $\mathcal{G}, \mathcal{H}$. Thus $\{\mathcal{L}_t\}$ is bounded below.

**(Part 3: Finite Termination)**
By Assumption 5, the permutation space $\Pi$ is finite for bounded $\mathcal{G}$. Since each accepted permutation produces a distinct graph $\mathcal{G}_{t+1} \neq \mathcal{G}_t$ (by strict inequality), and the number of distinct reachable graphs is finite, the algorithm must terminate in $T < \infty$ steps.

**(Part 4: Local Optimality)**
At termination ($t = T$), no permutation $\pi \in \Pi$ satisfies $\mathcal{L}(\pi(\mathcal{G}_T), \mathcal{H}_T) < \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_T, \mathcal{H}_T)$. By definition, $\mathcal{G}_T$ is a local optimum with respect to single-step permutations. $\square$

## Proof 2: Complexity Bound (Theorem 2)

**Bottleneck Analysis:** A single traversal of the DAG to compute per-node profiling metrics requires visiting each node and edge once: $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{V}| + |\mathcal{E}|)$.

**Permutation Proposal:** Candidate permutations are generated by sorting nodes by their profiling score (bottleneck severity) and selecting the top-$k$ subgraph neighborhoods. Sorting is $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{V}| \log |\mathcal{V}|)$.

**SMT Verification:** By Lemma 2, the verification of a bounded subgraph with $w$ quantum nodes costs $\mathcal{O}(2^w \cdot |\mathcal{V}_{sub}|)$.

**Digital Twin Simulation:** A forward pass through the cloned DAG to predict resource consumption: $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{V}| + |\mathcal{E}|)$.

The dominant terms are the sort ($|\mathcal{V}| \log |\mathcal{V}|$) and the SMT step ($2^w \cdot |\mathcal{V}_{sub}|$). Since $w \leq 20$ by Assumption 2 and $|\mathcal{V}_{sub}| \ll |\mathcal{V}|$, the SMT cost is effectively a bounded constant in practice. $\square$

## Proof 3: Correctness of Verified Evolution (Theorem 3)

This proof relies on the **soundness** property of SMT solvers.

**Definition (Soundness):** An SMT solver is *sound* if whenever it returns SAT for a formula $\phi$, the formula $\phi$ is indeed satisfiable; and whenever it returns UNSAT, the formula is indeed unsatisfiable.

**Construction:** For graphs $\mathcal{G}$ and $\mathcal{G}' = \pi(\mathcal{G})$, the Verifier constructs a formula:
$$\phi_{\text{equiv}} := \exists x.\ \text{Output}(\mathcal{G}, x) \neq \text{Output}(\mathcal{G}', x)$$

If the SMT solver returns UNSAT for $\phi_{\text{equiv}}$, then no counterexample exists, meaning the graphs are semantically equivalent.

By soundness, UNSAT $\implies$ $\mathcal{G} \equiv \mathcal{G}'$. Therefore, any permutation accepted by the Verifier preserves program semantics. $\square$
